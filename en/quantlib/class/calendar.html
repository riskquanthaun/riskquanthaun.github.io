# QuantLib-Python: Calendar Class

QuantLib Guide | 2025.01.18

Calendar Class

  • The QuantLib Calendar class plays an important role in date calculations in financial markets, helping to accurately process dates based on business days and holidays.

  • Since each financial market has unique business days and holidays, this class supports calendars for various financial markets like New York, London, Tokyo, etc., and enables accurate date calculations by reflecting each region's or market's business day conventions.

  • It can calculate the number of business days within a given period, calculate dates after specific business days, and verify whether specific dates are business days or not.

# Calendar Class Overview

One of the challenging aspects in OTC derivatives is holiday handling. Holiday handling is complicated because holidays vary greatly between countries, and business days frequently change due to irregular holidays like election days or alternative holidays.

Since the financial market is basically only open and quotes are formed on business days, if these holiday handling details are not properly managed, pricing results can become distorted. Therefore, QuantLib has a class for this holiday handling, which is the Calendar class.

# Calendar Class Structure

The Calendar class provides functionality to handle business days or holidays of specific exchanges or countries for pricing and calculates business days. To implement the Calendar class, you just need to input the name of the country you want to use.

The source code below shows examples of implementing calendars for the United States, Eurozone, Korea, Japan, and China as objects. The Calendar class is very useful because you don't need to make separate efforts to manage holidays for each country.


import QuantLib as ql

# Construction
us = ql.UnitedStates(ql.UnitedStates.NYSE)
uk = ql.UnitedKingdom(ql.UnitedKingdom.Metals)
eu = ql.TARGET()
kr = ql.SouthKorea()
jp = ql.Japan()
cn = ql.China()

# Calendar Class Functions

# some commonly used member functions:

import QuantLib as ql

cal = ql.SouthKorea()
date = ql.Date(20, 6, 2024)

# isBusinessDay(d)
# A Boolean value that determines whether d is a business day
print('Is BusinessDay :', cal.isBusinessDay(date))  # Is BusinessDay : True

# isHoliday(d)
# A boolean value that determines whether d is a holiday
print('Is Holiday :', cal.isHoliday(date))  # Is Holiday : False

 # isWeekend(w)
 # A Boolean value that determines whether w is a weekend (in some countries, weekends are not scheduled on Saturdays and Sundays)
print('Is Weekend :', cal.isWeekend(ql.Friday))  # Is Weekend : False

 # isEndOfMonth(d)
 # A boolean value that determines whether d is the last working day at the end of the month
print('Is Last BusinessDay :', cal.isEndOfMonth(ql.Date(27, 6, 2024)))  # Is Last BusinessDay : False

 # endOfMonth(d)
 # date, returns the last working day of the month in which d is located
print('Last BusinessDay :', cal.endOfMonth(date))  # Last BusinessDay : June 28th, 2024

# Holiday List

The holidayList() method takes a calendar (Calendar), start date (Date), and end date (Date) as arguments and returns the holidays that exist in the period between the start and end dates based on that calendar.

 # ql.Calendar.holidayList(calendar, from, to, includeWeekEnds=False)
print(ql.Calendar.holidayList(ql.SouthKorea(),
                              ql.Date(1,1,2024),
                              ql.Date(31,12,2024)))

# Custom Holiday List

The Calendar class supports calendar modification functionality because alternative holidays and irregular holidays are not monitored.

Calendar Class Functions

  • addHoliday(d): Method to add a new holiday
  • removeHoliday(d): Method to remove a holiday
import QuantLib as ql

cal = ql.TARGET()
  
day1 = ql.Date(26, 2, 2020)
day2 = ql.Date(10, 4, 2020)

print('Is Business Day : ', cal.isBusinessDay(day1))
## Is Business Day : True
print('Is Business Day : ', cal.isBusinessDay(day2))  
## Is Business Day : False

cal.addHoliday(day1)
cal.removeHoliday(day2) 

print('Is Business Day : ', cal.isBusinessDay(day1))  
## Is Business Day : False
print('Is Business Day : ', cal.isBusinessDay(day2))  
## Is Business Day : True

# Calculation Functions

The Calendar class provides functionality to calculate the number of business days between two dates and return a date after a specific period (Period) from a specific date (Date).

advance(d, period, convention, endOfMonth)

  • Returns the date after a specific period (Period) from a specific date (Date)

Business Day Convention

  • Criteria for determining the actual maturity date when the theoretical maturity date coincides with a holiday
  • Business day conventions are enumerated with several commonly used market standards, with each standard as follows

BusinessDayConvention

  • ql.Unadjusted: No adjustment
  • ql.Preceding: Adjust to the previous business day
  • ql.ModifiedPreceding: Adjust to the previous business day, but if the month changes, adjust to the next business day
  • ql.Following: Adjust to the next business day
  • ql.ModifiedFollowing: Adjust to the next business day, but if the month changes, adjust to the previous business day

End of Month

  • The end of month criterion determines whether to consistently end a one-month period on the last business day when the reference date is the last business day of the month.
  • For example, if today is February 29, 2024, it would be the last business day of February.
  • If the end of month is True, the date two months later would be April 30 (the last business day of April) rather than April 28.

businessDaysBetween(from, to, includeFirst, includeLast)

  • Takes a start date (Date) and end date (Date) as arguments and returns the number of business days between the two dates
import QuantLib as ql

cal = ql.SouthKorea()
firstDate = ql.Date(31, 1, 2024)
secondDate = ql.Date(1, 4, 2024)

print('Date 2 Adj :', cal.adjust(secondDate, ql.Preceding))  
## Date 2 Adj : April 1st, 2024
print('Date 2 Adj :', cal.adjust(secondDate, ql.ModifiedPreceding))  
## Date 2 Adj : April 1st, 2024

mat = ql.Period(2, ql.Months)  

print('Date 1 Month Adv :', cal.advance(firstDate, mat, ql.Following, False))  
## Date 1 Month Adv : April 1st, 2024
print('Date 1 Month Adv :', cal.advance(firstDate, mat, ql.ModifiedFollowing, False))  
## Date 1 Month Adv : March 29th, 2024

print('Business Days Between :',
    cal.businessDaysBetween(
        ql.Date(5, ql.March, 2024), ql.Date(30, ql.March, 2024), True, True
    )
)  ## Business Days Between : 19

# joint Calendar

Let's say a trader in Korea is trading an OTC derivative with a European trader using the 2-year U.S. Treasury rate as the underlying asset. In this case, the traders need to consider three calendars: Korea, Eurozone, and United States. They need to determine interest payment dates, rate setting dates, or maturity dates considering the holidays of all three countries.

jointCalendar() serves to combine individual calendars into one. jointCalendar() takes the calendars of needed countries as arguments and combines them to create a new unified calendar.

import QuantLib as ql

# Construction
us = ql.UnitedStates(ql.UnitedStates.NYSE)
eu = ql.TARGET()
kr = ql.SouthKorea()
 
date1 = ql.Date(1, 1, 2024)
date2 = ql.Date(31, 12, 2024)

 # ql.JointCalendar(calendar1, calendar2,
 #                  calendar3, JointCalendarRule=JoinHolidays)
new_calendar = ql.JointCalendar(us, eu, kr)
print(new_calendar.holidayList(date1, date2))
